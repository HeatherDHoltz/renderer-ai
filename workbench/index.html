PROCESS & REFERENCES

        
      

      
Render journal

      

        This project started with a Rust ray tracer and a question: how do you
        make a gemstone look convincing? The workbench grew from those material
        experiments. Here are a few of the renders along the way.
      

      
View the railcar and gemstone gallery ↗ · Scene images work without interactive graphics.

      

        
        
        
      

      

        
The browser version

        

          The workbench uses Three.js. The faceted stones trace light through
          their cuts, including internal reflections, absorption, and a small
          separation of red, green, and blue. Tiger’s eye uses a faster surface
          material. Ruby and sapphire colors are fitted to published absorption
          measurements; the other colors are visual studies.
        

        

          Some shortcuts keep the scene interactive. Reflections use captures of
          the room. Refracted light on the desk is calculated for a flat surface
          and doesn’t account for every object in its path. The loupe enlarges the
          camera view rather than simulating a complete optical lens.
        

        
The room

        

          The railcar was modeled in Blender, with its fixed lighting baked in
          Cycles. The source textures keep their full resolution. The ring,
          gemstone cases, and loupe remain interactive: drag the ring to turn it,
          open a case to change materials, or click the loupe to inspect the stone.
