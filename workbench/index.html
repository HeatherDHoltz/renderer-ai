BEHIND THE SPARKLE

One curiosity. Three ways to render it.

This bench grew out of Heather’s Rust ray tracer. The live viewer is a separate Three.js GPU material study; the images below are actual offline renders.

About the live bench

The tiger’s-eye shader combines procedural fine fibers, a view- and light-dependent directional highlight, and a single polished dielectric surface. The faceted gems trace refraction and internal reflection through their actual facets, with RGB dispersion and absorption. A live reflection probe captures the room and movable lamp for the jewelry. This remains an environment-based approximation without caustics or full scene path tracing. The seated view follows the cursor within a small limit while the jewelry turns independently. The loupe lifts from the bench along a clear path before the camera leans into its 3D brass eyepiece. Its magnification comes from the camera projection, rather than a full optical lens simulation. These are real-time approximations. Ruby and sapphire use three-channel fits to published absorption measurements; the other color presets are appearance studies.

The contemporary room is project-modeled in Blender, with Cycles-baked fixed lighting. Jewelry, velvet cases, task lamp and loupe remain live. Photographed velvet and HDR panoramas are credited to Poly Haven. The shank adapts Paul Wortmann’s CC0 ring band; the setting and live gemstone cuts are project-created.
